# Anatomy references

- Smithsonian Ocean: https://ocean.si.edu/ocean-life/invertebrates/giant-squid — eight arms, two extended tentacles, eye and mantle proportions, ventral funnel, central beak.
- Tree of Life, Architeuthis: https://tolweb.org/Architeuthis — small ovoid fins without free anterior lobes, two arm sucker series, four club series with larger medial suckers, carpal suckers and locking knobs, sparse stalk suckers/knobs, absence of photophores. Historical maximum size claims on this page are not used.
- NOAA: https://oceanservice.noaa.gov/facts/light_travel.html — twilight at 200–1,000 m; no sunlight below 1,000 m.

Model is procedural, not specimen-derived. Approximately 2.6 scene units/m: mantle ~2 m, arms ~2.1–2.4 m, feeding tentacle centerlines ~7 m before bending. Dimensions illustrate plausible relative proportions rather than a measured specimen.

Sucker microstructure, fine serrations, internal anatomy, pigmentation behavior, movement, optical attenuation and light scattering are simplified. Gentle mantle pulses/fin ripples are illustrative, not a validated hydrodynamic or behavioral simulation.

## Beak detail revision

Upper and lower beak shell geometry is informed by specimen photographs (Young, Bolstad & Vecchione) at https://tolweb.org/notes/?note_id=5453 and https://tolweb.org/notes/?note_id=5452. These source images are reference only and are not redistributed in the website. The hooked upper rostrum, broader lower jaw, thin supporting surfaces, dark tips, and surrounding buccal lips are modeled procedurally. Jaw articulation and lip folds are illustrative; this is not a specimen reconstruction or validated kinematics. Sucker denticles are a schematic repeating ring, not a taxonomic tooth-count claim.
