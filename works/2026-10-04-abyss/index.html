FIELD NOTES / 001

Built around the biology.

Architeuthis dux has a long muscular mantle, small ovoid fins at its rear, and large eyes embedded in the sides of its head. A ventral funnel expels water for jet propulsion.

Eight grasping arms carry two rows of suckers on their inner surfaces. Two much longer feeding tentacles end in narrow clubs: four main sucker rows, enlarged medial suckers, and a dense wrist region. Small locking suckers and knobs also run along the stalks. The paired chitinous beak sits inside the arm crown. Its dark cutting tips project from a muscular buccal mass; broader, thinner supporting walls extend into the surrounding tissue.

At 850 m, natural light is extremely faint. Here the red-brown skin is revealed by simulated ROV floodlights. Switch them off to see the difference; no sunlight is modeled at 1,000 m and deeper.

There is also a playful detour: tap the squid three times to turn it into giant ikayaki at an Okinawa-inspired beach barbecue. Use “Back to the deep” to return. The cookout is an imagined scene.

An anatomy-informed procedural model, not a scan of a specimen. Body proportions are illustrative; posture, movement, and microscopic structure remain simplified. The jaw-opening control illustrates the two parts; it is not a validated model of the feeding mechanics. Sucker denticles are schematic. Giant squid do not have light-producing photophores.
